The chord buttons ship as major, minor, suspended, and diminished, with **6th, dominant
7th, major 7th, and 9th** extensions — four of each. **All of them are editable.** Open the keyboard's settings sheet and choose
**Chords** or **Extensions**, then tap the button you want to change.

## Editing a chord

You get a keyboard for the slot. Tap notes on and off to build the voicing.

- The **root is always on** and cannot be tapped off.
- Build all chords in the key of C. Transposition happens automatically on the keyboard, not in the chord builder.
- A chord holds up to **five notes**. At five the board dims as one piece and no sixth will land.
  Remove one and there is room again.
- The confirm button is disabled for a chord of **one note** — a single note is not a chord — and
  enabled for anything else.
- **Cancel discards.** Re-opening the editor shows the stored slot, not the draft you abandoned.

## Editing an extension

An extension is exactly **one note**, always. The note that is currently on cannot be tapped off;
tap a different note and it moves there.

## Resetting

Each section has its own **Reset to defaults**, which appears only while that section has been
changed, and clears only that section. The app's **Reset user preferences** clears both.

Your edits survive a relaunch. If one of the two lists ever becomes unreadable, it falls back to
its own defaults without taking the other section with it.

## With VoiceOver

The editor keyboard is announced in **pitch order** — C, C♯, D — with each key's degree name and
its on/off state. The locked root and any blocked keys read as dimmed, and each Reset button says
which section it resets.
